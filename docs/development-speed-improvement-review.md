# Development speed: what to improve

**Review window:** v16 VM setup started **3 November 2026, 20:46:28.720763 UTC**; evidence frozen **11 November 2026, 12:41:05 UTC**. [Detailed evidence, top 30 and counting rules](development-speed-improvement-evidence.md).

The main avoidable delay was repeated discovery, repair and coordination between stages, compounded by failures to continue authorized work. It was not simply slow provisioning. The earlier record includes an acknowledged **7h15 continuation gap** and an unnecessary Windows image-inspection prerequisite spanning **13h16**. Later, one stalled diagnostic accumulated **168 status/event reads across 14h20** before cancellation. Reporting the same blockage repeatedly did not resolve it.

Real execution also mattered. The latest RHEL update took **400.143s**, host setup **585.253s**, managed verification **19.138s**, and managed reboot **79.652s**. These are individual jobs, not days of development. The 2,358 events in host setup are neither 2,358 tests nor 2,358 reruns. At the freeze, the host had rebooted successfully; post-reboot verification and AAP v18 allocation were not yet evidenced. This review does not claim v18 completion.

## Priorities

1. **Continue from the actual last completed stage.** After a terminal result, inspect it and take the next already-authorized action. Preserve genuine decisions about access, terms and destructive scope. Repeatedly asking for authority already granted, ending with another status recap, or mistaking an old task state for a fresh blocker adds delay. Use existing Beads records and normal templates; another dispatcher, action store or reminder loop would add maintenance rather than solve this.

2. **Give long operations realistic phase budgets and a response to stalled progress.** Conversion job 3517 exhausted its 7,200-second Controller budget after roughly 1h55 of preparation; its remote async conversion succeeded 31 seconds after Controller failure. That was a timeout-design defect. Reconcile remote outcome, credential cleanup and boot state before continuing; retain the failed job truthfully. Do not rerun conversion or reset the host to obtain a green workflow. For stalled diagnostics, establish an expected duration and investigate meaningful progress instead of indefinitely repeating reads. The precise cause of diagnostic 3470 remains unknown.

3. **Test the boundaries that actually failed, then retry the affected stage.** Numeric/string handling, literal credential templates, collection behavior, route identity and workflow links repeatedly passed through incompatible producers and consumers. Exercise those contracts with the installed Ansible/collection version before a long replay. A focused regression after a real fix is useful. Repeating a whole suite or successful pipeline without a new change, failure or unresolved concern needs a concrete reason; documentation-only maintenance does not justify it.

4. **Keep one existing CaC authority and reconcile drift narrowly.** Custom deployed ownership/JSON machinery and the abandoned artifact-key transport proposal created additional design and handoff work. Prefer the maintained CaC and existing normal stages. The latest example is simpler: three downstream templates still select generation 16 while their maintained capture is blank. After normal allocation completes, reconcile only those selectors to its actual result, preserving credentials, scope and guards. No new template or survey is required.

5. **Batch maintenance and consider repeated dependency work separately.** Beads, README, decision history and recovery mirrors are owner requirements, not automatically waste. Batch their required updates around meaningful checkpoints and avoid rereading unchanged state between them. Four recent project dependencies cost **411.525s**. The first two fetched different revisions; refreshing after a publication was justified. Two later repeats consumed the same revision and cost **177.364s**. Any reuse policy would need explicit revision/dependency guarantees; this is a proposal, not measured savings or an applied caching change.

Keep independent identity checks, production exclusions, secret protection, genuine approvals, cleanup verification and pre/post-reboot proof. Historical CCNG work was parallel; its withdrawn deployment and held infrastructure/tracker work are not current v18 gates. The improvement is fewer avoidable transitions and repeated deliberations, with the necessary evidence still intact.
